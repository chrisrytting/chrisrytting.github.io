Learning Loop is a philosophy and a practice. It helps you choose how to think, act, and live.

# LL as a philosophy
You as a person are singularly equipped to discover what to do to live the life you want. Every thing you hear on how to live life well--from books, scientific studies, religion, conventional wisdom, or any other source--is only a proposal that you yourself will have to validate as to its truth and appropriateness for you. Only you can feel internally what feels right, what feels wrong, what feels good and what feels bad. You are the agent of all agents, and effectively the central repository and locus of all truth.

Therefore, you must pay attention to what resonates in your life: 

#1. What works, what doesn't work, what may work

to

#2. Accomplish the goals, solve the problems, and answer the questions you most value.

And then you must not forget it.

**But how?**
# LL as a practice
You can achieve the above by following these 3 steps
- Recording what resonates (what you don't want to forget, what you feel matters to keep in mind, what you feel will)
- Organizing and processing these records so that the version of you that needs it can find it
- Using the resulting system of wisdom to guide your decisions and actions

and looping through them repeatedly, each loop refining how well you do the next.

# An example
If this all sounds very high level, here's a concrete example.

Let's say I struggle with my quality of life because I sleep poorly. Then I hear someone say "You should get 10 minutes of sunlight in your eyes first thing in the morning. That will help you sleep at night", and I am willing to run this experiment because the person is a famous Stanford researcher who studies sleep.

I try this, and I sleep lots better than I usually do.

I have uncovered a tool and a wisdom that I shouldn't forget, because it solves, for me specifically, a problem I care about.

I should have this wisdom written down, stored somewhere I can find it, and then use it to guide my decisions and actions.

For example, I might have a note/file I create called `sleep.md` that says


> [!NOTE]
> # Morning Routine
> # Sleep
> - Improve sleep by
>   - Getting 10 minutes of light exposure ASAP in the morning (per Dr. Andrew Huberman's podcast)



and I might know that I will almost never proactively look at that note, so that wisdom is not in a very effective place. So I put it in a different note, called `morning_routine.md`

> [!NOTE]
> # Morning Routine
> - [ ] Drink 16oz water
> - [ ] Get 10 minutes of light exposure ASAP (Huberman lab podcast)
> - [ ] Pray 5 minutes

If I look at my Morning Routine note every morning, now I will always use that tool / wisdom I was lucky enough to discover.
